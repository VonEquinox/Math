# A finite, computer-assisted exact result for regular polygons of sizes 25–32

**Computer-assisted theorem.** For every \(25\le n\le32\), the nonnegative rank of the regular \(n\)-gon slack matrix is \(10\). For every convex polygon with at least \(27\) vertices, not necessarily regular, a bounded extension needs at least \(10\) facets. These results do not settle the general formula; after the existing small-size results, the next critical instance is \(n=33\).

## 1. Reduction to dual sections of 4- and 5-polytopes

Let \(P\) be a polygon with the origin in its interior, vertices \(p_j\) and irredundant facets \(a_i\cdot x\le1\). Write \(S_{ij}=1-a_i\cdot p_j\), of ordinary rank 3. The polar is \(\operatorname{conv}\{a_i\}\); \(S^T\) is its slack matrix. Positive probability vectors on the vertices of \(P\) and of \(P^\circ\) have barycenter zero (use interiority and give each vertex a sufficiently small positive mass). Hence there are positive \(\lambda,\mu\) with \(S\lambda=\mathbf1\), \(\mu^TS=\mathbf1^T\).

If \(S=UV\) has \(r\le9\) summands, remove any inactive ones. Then the affine slice \(\operatorname{aff}\{V_{:j}\}\cap\mathbb R_+^r\) is bounded because the positive functional \(\mu^TU\) equals 1 throughout. Its dimension is \(\operatorname{rank}(V)-1\), it has at most \(r\) facets, and its image under \(U\) is exactly the slack realization of \(P\) (the image is in the affine slack slice, is nonnegative, and contains the vertex images). The same construction applied to \(S^T\) has dimension \(\operatorname{rank}(U)-1\) and projects to \(P^\circ\). Sylvester gives sum of dimensions at most \(r+1\le10\).

By the upper bound theorem, a bounded polytope with at most eight facets has at most 20 vertices, and one of dimension at most three with at most nine facets has at most 14. Thus when \(n\ge25\), \(r=9\), both dimensions are at least four, and at least one is four or five. After translating the lift of the planar origin to an interior point of the extension (linear projection maps relative interiors onto relative interiors), polar duality gives an interior two-plane section of a 9-vertex polytope of dimension four or five. For a regular \(P\), either resulting planar polygon is regular up to rotation and positive uniform scaling.

## 2. Finite generic section enumeration

Translate the section center to zero. Perturb the nine vertices generically while keeping the section plane fixed. Each vertex remains strictly exposed with a margin; interiority and an origin ball persist. The two-dimensional section converges in Hausdorff distance, because for every small \(\epsilon>0\) eventually \((1-\epsilon)Q\subset Q_\nu\subset(1+\epsilon)Q\), proved via support functions and a uniform origin ball. It cannot lose any of the original section's separate exposed vertices. In particular it has at least 25 vertices if the original does. The generic perturbation makes every facet simplicial and the projection onto the transverse quotient uniform (any \(d-2\) vectors linearly independent). Consequently each section corner is a hit in the relative interior of a \((d-2)\)-dimensional ridge, detected by whether the quotient vectors of its \(d-1\) vertices positively contain zero.

For generic quotient halfspaces through zero, both complementary induced complexes of vertices have Euler characteristic 1. Indeed a boundary cap is an open ball by radial projection about zero; simplicial barycentric coordinates retracted onto positive-vertex coordinates, by dividing by their positive sum and straight-line interpolation, give a deformation retraction to the induced complex. This condition is necessary, not asserted sufficient.

- In dimension four the transverse quotient has rank 2. Every signed projective circular order is a half-rotation that toggles each of nine labels once; after quotienting rotation, sign and reversal, it is finite. Positive triples are exactly those whose signs along their projective order alternate. Over the 262 simplicial candidate types having ≥25 facets, neither of two checker implementations permits a ≥25-corner section. See ROUND2_GEOMETRY.md for the input and controls. Its limit argument also excludes all nongeneric 4D hypotheses.
- In dimension five the transverse quotient has rank 3. Contract it by the direction of any one vertex: the other eight labels give a rank-2 signed projective order. Every halfspace cut along this contraction can perturb the sign of the omitted vertex in either direction, so both options on both complementary sides must pass the Euler test. Each ambient facet containing a genuine section edge must have its non-omitted vertices on both sides of each cut: an interior point of that facet belongs to the section with all facet barycentric weights positive, and the omitted vertex is on the cutting plane. Thus every cut leaves at least 25 possible section facets. The checker keeps all such signed orders, and glues the nine rank-2 contractions whenever their shared signs agree on overlapping triples. The only sign quotient of the glued table is its common global negation. Actual uniform quotient data always occur in this overestimate; imposing oriented-matroid realizability is unnecessary.
- A tetrahedral ridge on four labels is hit exactly when the four corresponding triple determinant signs alternate in the sorted order; the signed cofactors give its unique linear dependence. Hit ridges join the two ambient facets containing them. For a true section this graph is one polygon, with vertices corresponding to intersected facets and edges to hit ridges. The enumeration returns 8,202 qualifying complete type/table pairs (8,190 with 25 corners, 12 with 26) and **no genuine-section candidate with more than 26 corners**. The qualifying pairs give 298 distinct ordered support templates (294 of length 25, four of length 26). The cardinalities of the published complete simplicial classifications are explicit dependencies. The archive gives exact integer witnesses for all 1,142 and 322 pairwise nonisomorphic types in dimensions 4 and 5 respectively, so the replay need not trust the completeness or correctness of a downloaded face-list mirror. A second exhaustive contraction checker agrees on every rank-two record, and a separate dynamic constraint solver agrees on every glued table and section. No numerical frequency or sampling is used to claim completeness.
  
Therefore no 9-facet extension of a polygon with at least 27 vertices exists in either dimension available by rank balance. This already handles sizes 27–32 with the known 10-term upper bound.

## 3. Exclude the 25- and 26-corner regular cases, including degenerate limits

For a generic section every corner \(p_j^{(\nu)}\) lies in a tetrahedral ridge \(T_j\) on four external labels, so take a barycentric representation supported on \(T_j\) whose weights sum to one. Each section edge lies in an ambient facet \(F_i\) on five labels. Write its ambient facet inequality as \(1-\eta_i^{(\nu)}\cdot x\ge0\) and evaluate it on the nine ambient vertices; the resulting row mask is contained in the complement of \(F_i\). The two factors multiply to the section slack matrix. Uniform interior balls imply that each ambient \(\eta_i^{(\nu)}\) is uniformly bounded. Similarly, projected normals of supporting edges are bounded away from zero because each projected edge has a point in a uniform-radius ball and its constant is one.

In the generic approximations independently choose a corner near every original corner, using distinct strictly exposing neighborhoods. Also choose an edge whose normal and constant converge to each of the original edge data: apply the same exposure argument to the polar sections, which converge by the same uniform ball and Hausdorff bounds. All assignments can preserve cyclic order; relabel regular indices by simultaneous rotations/reflections. If the original is a regular 25-gon and the approximation has 26 corners, allow **independent deletion of one corner and one edge**, not necessarily incident. Keeping the maximum allowed support may place a term at a zero of the *limit*, so do not use that as a rejection: the actual nonnegative factors take a bounded convergent subsequence, and their supports may shrink. After taking a subsequence of the finite templates and cyclic alignments, the limiting factorization gives exactly the desired regular slack matrix up to common or positive row/column scaling. Allowed labels at positive cells are therefore upper masks for the terms of a putative exact factorization.

There are two sound terminal rejection rules:
1. A positive cell with no allowable label cannot be represented.
2. If two positive cells \((i,j),(k,l)\) are each permitted **only** the same latent label, that summand must equal the two full slack values there, so
   \(S_{ij}S_{kl}\le S_{il}S_{kj}\)
   by the rank-one product identity and nonnegative domination at the crossing cells. A strict reverse inequality rejects the template. This logic is unchanged by row and column scaling or by the presence of permissive structural zeros elsewhere.

The strict comparisons use exact real cyclotomic product orders; n=25 has a separate rational interval implementation and n=26 a separate terminal checker as cross-checks:
- S25: 149,900 independent row/column deletion and dihedral alignments; 149,190 have a positive hole, the remaining 710 violate a forced-private product; zero remain.
- S26: 208 alignments from its four 26-corner templates; 204 have a hole, the remaining four violate the product; zero remain.

The end-stage scripts and certificates keep these cases distinct from any SAT experiment. Known exact fold upper bounds give rank ≤10 and were independently checked for all eight sizes via polynomial identities of all matrix entries. Combining with the foregoing excludes ≤9 and gives **rank 10 for n=25,…,32**.

## 4. Offline replay

Run sh CODE/r2_replay_exact_25_32.sh from the archive root. It regenerates both catalogs of facet masks from exact integer data, checks the published cardinalities by verifying pairwise nonisomorphism, runs two 4D enumerations, and compares the independent 5D contraction and gluing implementations. It then generates all ordered templates, checks the final private-product comparison both by directed intervals (n=25) and by exact minimal-polynomial root isolation (n=25 and n=26), and verifies the known 10-term decomposition for each size 25–32. It does not require a SAT solver, commercial software, or a network connection. The final two 5D screens also have a positive control sourced from a genuine 24-sided exact rank-nine construction, recorded separately in the certificate files.
